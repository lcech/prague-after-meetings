“Historic” car rides

A vintage-looking vehicle does not necessarily mean a historic car or a historically meaningful tour. These rides are not part of the ordinary public-transport network.

For this short outing, put the money towards a meal and use public transport instead. If you still want a ride, confirm the operator, vehicle, exact route, duration, and total price before paying. A themed ride is a choice, not automatically a scam.

Poor-value currency exchange

“Zero commission” does not guarantee a good exchange rate. Ask how many koruna you will receive for the exact amount you hand over, compare offers, and keep the receipt. Never exchange money with someone who approaches you in the street.

At a payment terminal or ATM, choosing CZK and declining the machine's offered home-currency conversion usually lets your own card provider handle conversion. Your bank may still charge fees. Read ATM withdrawal fees separately and cancel if the total is poor value.

Trdelník: enjoy it if you like, but know what it is

The sugar-coated pastry sold around central Prague is heavily marketed to visitors. It has a broader Central European history, but is not a defining traditional Prague dessert. Ice-cream-filled versions are a modern tourist offering.

It is not inherently unsafe or a scam. For the Czech bakery experience this guide prioritises, look for buchty, apple štrůdl, or seasonal vánočka instead. Compare the price before ordering any treat beside a major attraction.

Paying for the address rather than the meal

A terrace facing a famous sight may charge a premium for the location. That is not automatically dishonest, but it may not be the best fit for a food-first afternoon. Read the menu and drink prices before sitting down, check extras, and choose somewhere whose food interests you.
