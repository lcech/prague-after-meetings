Something Czech, not just something with “Prague” on it

For a short visit, edible gifts are often the easiest choice. Look for a clear producer, sealed packaging, ingredients, and a sensible best-before date. A familiar Czech product from an ordinary shop can be a better buy than a souvenir box sold beside a landmark.

Lázeňské oplatky

Thin, round spa wafers, traditionally associated with Czech spa towns. They are light, easy to share, and available in different fillings. They are a Czech gift rather than a specifically Prague invention.

Choose a sealed box and pack it where it will not be crushed. Check ingredients for wheat, milk, and nuts, depending on the variety.

Žufánek slivovice

A gift for an adult who enjoys spirits: slivovice, a plum distillate associated especially with Moravia. Žufánek is a family distillery in the White Carpathians region; its product uses the name Slivovica and is listed at 50% ABV.

Use the producer's website to explore the range and confirm the product. Do not assume the distillery is in Prague or that a nearby souvenir shop has stock. Arrange a verified retailer or delivery before making a special trip.

Check the departure airport's current cabin-liquid rules, airline baggage rules, and your destination's alcohol import allowances before buying. A full-size bottle may need checked baggage. Wrap it securely and keep it sealed.

Prefer a non-alcoholic gift?

Spa wafers are the simplest starting point. For something longer lasting, consider a small Czech-made design object or toy, checking the maker and country of manufacture rather than relying on the souvenir label. Specific shops will be shortlisted separately.
