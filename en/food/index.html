Order something Czech

Czech pub cooking is hearty, often built around meat, sauces, cabbage, and dumplings. One main course may be plenty. Bread dumplings are slices served alongside the dish, not filled dumplings.

Three dishes to look for

On the menu What arrives Why try it?
Svíčková na smetaně Beef with a creamy root-vegetable sauce and bread dumplings, often with cranberry garnish. A distinctive Czech combination of savoury, creamy, and lightly sweet flavours. The name does not guarantee a particular premium cut of beef.
Guláš A rich, usually beef-based stew with onion and paprika, commonly served with bread dumplings. A comforting pub classic. The Czech version is usually a substantial stew rather than a thin soup.
Vepřo-knedlo-zelo Roast pork, dumplings, and cabbage or sauerkraut. A classic Czech plate whose nickname tells you its three main parts.

Menus change. These are dishes to recognise, not a promise that every restaurant serves all three every day.

Leave room for something sweet

  • Štrůdl: apple strudel, often with cinnamon and sometimes raisins or nuts. Familiar across Central Europe and firmly at home in Czech cafés and kitchens.
  • Buchty: soft yeast buns with fillings such as poppy seed, plum butter, or sweet curd cheese. A lovely bakery find; fillings and availability vary.
  • Vánočka: a braided, enriched sweet loaf, traditionally associated with Christmas. It may contain raisins and almonds. Some bakeries sell it beyond the festive season, but do not count on it everywhere year-round.

These are good reasons to look in a bakery rather than choosing a dessert only because it is advertised as an “Old Prague” speciality.

Where to eat

Lokál U Bílé kuželky

Best fit for the suggested afternoon. A Czech pub in Malá Strana, at Míšeňská 12, near Charles Bridge. Its central location means visitors and regulars share the same space; genuine food does not require an undiscovered address.

Book a table, check the current menu, and choose a Czech main that appeals to you. Do not assume a dish pictured in this guide will be available on the day.

Restaurant and reservations

Lokál Dlouhááá

An alternative for an Old Town afternoon. Keep this as a separate plan if you would rather combine your meal with Old Town Square. Check the restaurant's current menu and booking details before setting off.

Restaurant website

U Pinkasů

An alternative near Wenceslas Square. This long-established pub at Jungmannovo náměstí 15/16 serves Czech cuisine and Pilsner Urquell. Its published menu includes beef in cream sauce, beef goulash, and roast pork with cabbage and dumplings, making it a useful shortlist option for the dishes above.

Check the current menu and reserve indoor seating if you need a confirmed table. Do not assume the seasonal garden is open or bookable.

What to budget for food

As a concrete reference, U Pinkasů's published menu checked on 30 October 2026 lists beef goulash with dumplings at 339 CZK and a 0.47 l Pilsner Urquell at 82 CZK: 421 CZK together, before any optional tip or extras.

For planning, set aside around 450–650 CZK per person for a main course and a drink, with a little room for extras. This is a suggested allowance, not a quoted set menu or a verified price range for every restaurant. Dessert, additional drinks, transport, and gifts can increase the total. Check the restaurant's own current menu before booking.

Beer, without the homework

Ask for a small beer if you want to taste without committing to a large glass. A pale lager is an easy introduction. Czech beer labels such as 10° and 12° refer to the strength of the original wort, not the alcohol percentage; check ABV separately.

Foam is part of Czech beer service, not automatically a sign of a short pour. If you are curious, ask the staff about the pours they offer. There is no need to order a round of shots to complete the experience.

Alcohol is for adults aged 18 and over. Choose a soft drink or an alcohol-free beer if you prefer; check the label if you need strictly 0.0% alcohol.

Before you order

Ask about allergens and dietary needs. Dumplings and pastries commonly contain wheat, eggs, or milk; sauces may contain celery, cream, or other allergens. A vegetable side can still contain lard or meat stock. Descriptions here are not allergen guarantees.

Check whether sides are included in the main-course price. Ask for the bill when ready and check it before paying. Tipping is optional; if you leave one, make the final amount clear and first check whether service has already been included.
